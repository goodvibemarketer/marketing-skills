# Weekly Drop, w/c 13 April 2026

## Theme idea
Something about how the MEES regulations and the new TCFD mandatory reporting are pushing mid-sized landlords into retrofit decisions they keep trying to defer. The whole "we'll deal with it when the rules tighten" strategy is running out of runway.

## News stories to cover

1. **MEES 2030 consultation closed.** Government confirmed minimum EPC C rating for all commercial lets by 2027, then B by 2030. Penalty ceiling raised to £150k per breach. Link: gov.uk/consultation-mees-2027
   - Most mid-sized landlords still at D or E on a meaningful chunk of their portfolio
   - Private equity-owned portfolios hit hardest because they sweated assets

2. **British Land announced £1.5bn retrofit programme** across London estate, with Scope 3 reduction targets aligned to SBTi 1.5 degree pathway. Took the hit on earnings guidance.
   - Framing in their press release: "transition risk management" not "climate leadership"
   - Telling because that's the language investors now use

3. **TCFD mandatory for UK companies over 500 employees from April 2026 reporting.** FCA clarified that "transition plan" means a costed, scheduled programme, not a policy statement.
   - Auditors now pushing back on vague wording
   - KPMG client note leaked, big four will be refusing sign-offs on unquantified transition plans

4. **Heat pump grant increased to £12k for non-domestic** under the revised BUS scheme. But installation capacity is the bottleneck, not funding. Quote from HPA: "we could install double what we're installing if we had the engineers."

5. **Green Finance Institute report** showing 60% of commercial property loans due for refinancing in 2027 will fail the new MEES threshold without intervention. £36bn at risk.
   - Lenders starting to price climate risk into refinancing terms
   - Specific example: Lloyds adding 75bps to loans on non-compliant buildings

## Loose notes for the article

- Keep seeing this pattern where landlords treat retrofit as an environmental decision when it's really a capital markets decision
- MEES was supposed to be the big stick, but it's the debt refinancing squeeze that's actually moving people
- The interesting thing is that the "green premium" on rents is now real and measurable, BNP Paribas has data showing A-rated commercial space pulls 8% higher rent than C-rated equivalents in London
- This reframes the whole business case. It's not "retrofit costs money." It's "non-retrofit costs more money, and the cost is accelerating"
- Want to make the point that sustainability officers have been saying this for years but were ignored because they framed it as climate. Now CFOs are saying it and listening, because they're framing it as finance
- Possible title: something about how the retrofit conversation is moving from boiler rooms to boardrooms, or how the CFO has become the new sustainability officer

## Personal thread
First retrofit I worked on properly was a 1970s office block in Reading in 2018. We couldn't get board approval because the ROI was 12 years. Same building today would have the sign-off in a meeting because the downside case (non-compliance at refinancing) is now worse than the upside case. That shift took six years. Worth mentioning.
