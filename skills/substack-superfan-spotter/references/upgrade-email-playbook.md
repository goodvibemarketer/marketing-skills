# Upgrade email playbook

What the evidence says about turning free newsletter readers into paying ones, and how this Skill uses it. Researched 20 Nov 2026. Where a finding comes from a different context (sales outreach, SaaS), it says so.

## What to expect

- Substack's own guidance: expect to convert roughly **3–10% of your whole free list** to paid over time ([Going paid on Substack: a checklist](https://on.substack.com/p/going-paid-checklist)). Amy Suto puts healthy newsletters at 2–10% and her own at about 5% ([what converted her first 1,900](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/how-to-get-paid-subscribers-on-substack)).
- The superfan segment should convert well above the list average, because it's the most engaged part of it. Don't promise a number. Measure it: rerun the Skill a month later and count who upgraded.

## What to say

1. **Sell an outcome, not support.** "Support my work" converts only superfans; a paid tier with its own promise converts more (Suto). Mack Collier reports moving from 0.5% to 4% conversion after repositioning paid as "a defined outcome, not a tip jar", with a clear before and after ([Backstage Pass](https://mackcollier.substack.com/p/lets-add-a-conversion-strategy-to)). One writer's report, not a study, but it matches Substack's advice.
2. **Say what free gets and what they're missing.** Substack's advice for its unfinished-subscription email: explain what readers get for free and what they miss until they upgrade, in a personal tone ([Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-convert-new-readers-into-paying-subscribers-in-your-own-words)).
3. **Show, don't list.** Substack suggests emailing engaged free readers "teasers, unlocked posts, or limited-time discounts", and paywall previews with a subscribe button ([checklist](https://on.substack.com/p/going-paid-checklist), [free vs paid](https://on.substack.com/p/free-vs-paid)). One example from a real paid post beats five bullet points.
4. **Use real proof.** Testimonials and reviews are standard advice from Substack and Ghost ([free vs paid](https://on.substack.com/p/free-vs-paid), [Ghost](https://ghost.org/resources/conversion-magic/)). Only ever quote real readers, with permission. With no testimonials yet, use a concrete fact about the work (how many Skills, how often it's updated) instead.
5. **Business readers can expense it.** The Pragmatic Engineer tells readers they can expense the subscription as an educational resource ([free vs paid](https://on.substack.com/p/free-vs-paid)). Worth a line for professional audiences, worded as a possibility, not a promise.
6. **One clear price and one link.** Recommend one plan rather than a menu, to avoid decision fatigue ([Userlist](https://userlist.com/blog/upgrade-email/), SaaS context). Mention the annual saving in a single clause if there is one.

## Offers and deadlines

- Substack lets you create **special offers**: a discount (on the first year or for as long as they subscribe) or a **free trial**, shown to everyone or only to people with the private link ([Substack guide](https://on.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-gift-subscriptions-and), [Substack FAQ](https://faq.substack.com/p/how-to-create-special-offers-for)).
- Substack's data on offers: higher discounts generally convert better; **20% discounts produced some of the best conversion and click-through rates**, and 60%+ also did well. How the offer is presented matters more than the exact percentage (same guide).
- **Real deadlines convert; announcements alone don't** (Suto). Ghost says to use limited-time offers carefully ([Ghost](https://ghost.org/resources/conversion-magic/)). So: a deadline only if the offer really ends then.

## How many emails

- **One email is not enough.** Suto: send more than one. Ghost: make the ask often. In a study of 12 million sales-outreach emails, one follow-up increased replies by 65.8% ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study), outreach context, not newsletters).
- A short sequence spaced about a week apart, mostly soft with one harder ask, is the standard SaaS pattern ([Userlist](https://userlist.com/blog/upgrade-email/)). Take anyone who upgrades out before the next send.

## How it should look

- **Plain text.** In HubSpot's A/B tests, plain-text emails beat HTML ones: an HTML template with images got 51% fewer clicks, and even one image reduced the click rate ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/plain-text-vs-html-emails-data)). No images, no banners, one link or button.
- **Subject lines:** specific and honest, naming a result or benefit ([Userlist](https://userlist.com/blog/upgrade-email/)). In the outreach study, subject lines of 36–50 characters got the most replies, and personalised ones did 30.5% better (Backlinko). Substack can't put a name in the subject line, so personalise with what they did ("you've read most of…") rather than their name.
- **Ask a question.** Dan Oshinsky ends his welcome email with questions and replies to every answer, which starts conversations that turn into clients ([Ghost](https://ghost.org/blog/conversion-tactics)). A reply is a warmer signal than a click.

## Sending on Substack

- **Segments:** in Subscribers → Segments, save a filter (activity level, paid status, signup date and more) and either select the subscribers and click *Send email*, or notify only that segment from a post's Advanced publish settings ([How to Grow a Newsletter, Sept 2026](https://howtogrowanewsletter.substack.com/p/substack-just-launched-subscriber)).
- **Select and email:** in All Subscribers, filter, tick the readers and click *Email*. It goes as a direct email, not a post on your publication ([Substack support](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/19163529016212-How-do-I-send-an-email-to-one-or-a-select-group-of-subscribers-on-Substack)).
- **First names:** `%%first_name%%` in the body inserts each reader's first name. It's undocumented and could stop working, doesn't work in subject lines, and shows as blank if Substack has no name, so don't put a comma straight after it ([Really Good Business Ideas](https://www.reallygoodbusinessideas.com/p/substack-first-name-variable), [Me Time](https://metimepost.substack.com/p/these-2-features-are)).
- **Personal notes** to the very top readers still go from your own inbox. Write each by hand; with a small list, writers recommend that over templates ([Substack office hours](https://on.substack.com/p/office-hours-paid-subs-73)).
